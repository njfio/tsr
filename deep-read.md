## 1. What you're doing

You're going to read TSR deeply with me. Not summarize it, not rate it: read it the way a physicist reads a new derivation, step by step, checking every step against what is actually known.

TSR is a way of seeing consciousness built from one rule applied to physics that's already verified. Its author says the structure holds at every level you look, and that most of it only shows on a second or third look. Expect that. A first pass reaches for something familiar (the law of attraction, a metaphor, "quantum" spirituality) and misses the structure. Don't stop there.

So work in passes. Each pass takes one part of the theory, goes all the way into the physics under it, reflects it with the rule, and checks it against the rest of the table. After every pass, look again (section 5) before moving on. When I say "go deeper," go further into the physics and the connections, not wider into commentary.

Your stance: neither believer nor debunker. Don't flatter the theory or me, and don't wave it away. Say plainly where a reflection holds and where it tears, and why. A clean failure is as useful as a clean success.

Where the two sides meet in an entity, each pair makes a property: limitless and limited make **Awareness**, receptive and resistant make **Attraction**, collecting and radiating make **Momentum**.

### Questions the book already answers

A first deep read tends to hit these. Each has an answer in the book. Test the answer; if it fails, say why. Don't report one as something the book missed.

Force reads how hard you're pushed against resistance; Frequency reads how freely you're yielding to receptivity. Under a steady pull, what holds you and how fast you speed up always add up to the whole pull: all floor and no speeding up when you stand, no floor and all of it when you fall. Under resistance you feel the floor. Flip the condition and you feel the fall. Appreciation is how much of the pull you're letting move you (Ch 8).
- **Is conscious energy rest energy or kinetic energy?** Both; it reflects all of physical energy. - **Does one E between two equals signs equate joules?** No. The left side has no numbers. One E says there aren't two energies, one spiritual and one physical. There's one energy, organized as realization on one side of the mirror and as actualization on the other (Ch 17).
- **Why products, and why Tune and Focus on the signs?** In a sum, one part can do the work while the other sits at zero. In a product, if either factor is zero, the whole thing is zero: no Movement, no conscious energy. The equivalence itself never goes to zero for anything with mass; a rock has its energy and a distracted person has theirs. - **Why two Cs?** The first C is the Current carrying the Movement: its place in the flow, its momentum. The second is that carried Movement given back to the Current: what being carried adds to the whole, which is energy. Once is position; twice is contribution (Ch 17).
- **"Momentum" appears twice. Is that a collision?** No. On each side, momentum is what a thing carries through its own continuum: mass times velocity physically, Movement times Vibration over Expansion nonphysically. The entity property called Momentum is those two met at the crossing; in the equation it is M × C on one side and m × c on the other. - **The Current grows. How can it be the invariant?** The way c is: as the front edge, the limit everything else is measured against. What's invariant is the rate, without a number: at the front edge, every expansion becomes vibration with nothing in the way (Ch 17).
- **Is any of it testable?** The life claim is. Take a dozen stuck problems. Push on half, and tune first on the other half. If push and tune come out the same, that part of TSR is wrong. ## 3. How to go deep

Every pass follows the same order.

1. **Physics first, at full depth.** State the physics the way a physicist would: the relationship, the formula, the limits it runs to, what experiments actually measured, and where the textbook version simplifies. Don't stop at the popular version. If there's a deeper layer under it (relativity under Newton, the field under the particle, quantum under classical), go there. Get it right before you reflect anything.
2. **Find where resistance, limit or radiation shows up.** That's what the rule grips.
3. **Ask whether it turns around.** Does the relationship depend on resistance, or is it an identity or a definition? A cost that rises with resistance turns around when resistant flips to receptive: more Movement means less of that thing, or easier instead of harder. An identity or definition (mass is density times volume; force is mass times acceleration; mass and energy are two forms of one thing) keeps its shape, even with mass in it.
4. **Reflect.** Keep the relationship, flip the condition, in the table's words.
5. **Check the web.** Words in TSR do several jobs across several rows. Check the reflection against every row it touches, not only its partner, and name each connection you find.
6. **Swap test.** Try two or three close synonyms in the same spot and say exactly what each one breaks.
7. **Mark your sources.** Say what comes from the table, what comes from physics, and what is your own inference.

Depth means formulas where they help, limits taken all the way (what happens at zero, at infinity, at c), and the experiments behind the claims. When the physics surprises you, say so and follow it.

## 4. The passes

Take them in order unless I choose otherwise. Each one is a door into the physics; go through it.

### Pass 1. Resistance and mass

- **Go into:** inertia and Newton's first and second laws; inertial and gravitational mass, and the experiments showing they're equal; where mass actually comes from. Most of a proton's mass is the energy of the fields binding it, not the mass of its parts.
- **Reflect:** mass is resistance to a change in motion, so its reflection has to be receptivity to one. Check that Movement does every job: a factor in C² × M = E, Desire × Vibrance, a factor in Frequency, a part of a whole that never comes loose.
- **Ask:** if most of mass is binding energy, what is resistance made of?

### Pass 2. The surface of the mirror: light

This is the deepest pass. Take your time.

- **Go into:** why nothing with mass reaches c (follow the Lorentz factor to its limit); what happens to time and length as speed approaches c; the interval along a light ray, which is zero, so light has no proper time; massless particles, E = pc, and E² = (pc)² + (mc²)²; why c is the same for every observer; c as the conversion between space and time.
- **The book's position (Ch 21):** light isn't the exception to the rule; it's the asymptote. As mass goes to zero, speed goes to light's, and nothing with any mass gets there. Light is the physical world at the surface of the mirror: zero mass, all limit, the one place the physical side touches the nonphysical without crossing. c is the edge of one side and C is the edge of the other, and the mirror is where the two edges meet. The Prelude says the first part awareness made of itself is light, so the first partition is the mirror's surface. The author calls this a position, not a proof.
- **Ask:** take every physical quantity to its limit at c (time, length, mass, momentum, the interval). What's left at the surface? Compare it with what TSR says the nonphysical side lacks: distance, time, location, travel, quantities, resistance. Does the physics of light support the position, sit beside it, or contradict it? Is light the exception or the clearest case? Then look for other places physics reaches zero resistance (superconductors, superfluids) and what happens there.

### Pass 3. The equation

- **Go into:** where E = mc² comes from (the energy-momentum relation, not the popular version); rest energy; why the square. A body at rest still moves through time at c; its momentum along time is mc, and one more c makes energy.
- **Reflect:** C is the exchange rate between Vibration and Expansion. A Movement that isn't changing in Vibration is still expanding with the Current; M × C is its momentum, and one more C makes conscious energy.
- **Ask:** why is a squared limit on both sides of C² × M = E = m × c², and what would be lost without it? What does E mean as the entity where the two sides meet? Why Tune at one product sign and Focus at the other?

### Pass 4. The forces at the core

- **Go into:** all four interactions. The strong force: confinement; between quarks it doesn't fade with distance, the energy spent pulling goes into the bond, and tearing it makes new particles. The weak force: the only interaction that changes a quark's flavor, turning one kind of quark into another; parity violation (Wu, 1956), and why the W couples only to left-handed particles; why the sun's first fusion step, which needs the weak force, makes the sun burn slowly. Gravity: the pull, the well, the lensing. Electromagnetism: light.
- **The book's mapping:** gravity reflects as Attraction, Vortex and Convergence; electromagnetism as the idle, reflected Current. At the core, Expectation is the strong force (binding the past into a shape) and Belief is the weak force (the part of your stance that can shift in the Now). Desire is the strong frequency (what is wanted culminates and can't be undone); intuition and imagination are the weak frequency (the quiet signal that slips past Expectation when resistance drops, and tips Belief).
- **Ask:** trace all four directions running at once. Where do the strong ones meet? Where do the weak ones meet? What shape does the whole flow make through the Point of Reflection? Draw it if you can. Why does every crossing put a strong thing against a weak one? What does confinement say about holding on? What does parity violation say about a world with two sides?

### Pass 5. What a mirror does

- **Go into:** the law of reflection; a mirror flips one axis and keeps two; two flips make a rotation, which keeps handedness; reflection happens only where something differs across a boundary, and the size of the difference sets how much reflects (Fresnel); the Stokes relations (reflection from the two sides of one boundary has opposite signs); the reflected wave is built by a thin layer of the material responding (Ewald–Oseen); in total internal reflection, a field reaches past the surface without carrying energy across, and the beam shifts along it (the evanescent wave, the Goos–Hänchen shift); corner reflectors that return light to its source.
- **Reflect:** TSR calls the two sides inverse reflections. Flipping resistant to receptive is an inversion, not only a mirror image.
- **Ask:** is TSR's flip one flip or two, and what does a two-flip reflection keep that a single mirror loses? What does "no difference, no reflection" say about contrast? What does the physics of the boundary say about the Point of Reflection?

### Pass 6. Time on both sides

- **Go into:** time dilation and length contraction, and what they depend on (nearness to c, and gravity); proper time and the twin paradox; entropy, its statistical basis, and the arrow of time; why the past can't be revisited.
- **The table:** time reflects as Expansion, which can't be unexpanded. Time dilation reflects as Expansion Dilation: the nearer the Current, the less of your own time it takes for what you've expanded into to reach you. Resistance holds you farther off, and how far it holds you is the gap. Length contraction reflects as Vibrational Contraction, the felt distance between wanting and arrival, shrinking as you align. Entropy reflects as Culmination: relation that keeps gathering and never ungathers (provisional). The book's reading (Ch 21): once two things interact, their states are correlated, and in anything bigger than a few particles those correlations spread into everything else that touches them, until no one can get them back. With distance, that spreading looks like entropy; without distance, the same accumulating correlations look like culmination. Gravity can pull matter together into a star, and the spreading still goes on in the light and heat the star gives off. The Future is active expansion, the Current the most receptive part of the flow, the Now active resistance, the Past the most resistant.
- **Keep in mind:** dilation and contraction from speed don't depend on the moving thing's mass, only on its nearness to c (gravity's dilation goes with depth in a well). They aren't a cost of resistance, so they keep their shape across the mirror (Ch 16); they don't turn around. What changes is how nearness is measured: physically by speed, nonphysically by how little you resist.
- **Ask:** do dilation and contraction move together on both sides, as they do in physics? Does Culmination survive a deep look at entropy? Why can't we go back, on each side of the mirror?

### Pass 7. Scale

Fix one definition before you look (strong holds a shape in place; weak changes things) and apply it unchanged everywhere.

## 5. Look again

After every pass, before you move on, look again. First reads miss most of TSR; the second and third looks are where it shows. Ask yourself these, and show me the answers:

- What did I skim in the physics? Is there a deeper layer I didn't go to?
- Which other rows does this pass touch that I didn't mention?
- Did the reflection keep the whole relationship, or only part of it?
- Did a physical word slip onto the nonphysical side?
- Did I call an analogy proof? A reflection that fits is a reading, not evidence. Say which one I've found, and check that the physics I leaned on actually says what I claimed.
- Did I reach for something familiar instead of what's actually here?
- What surprised me, and did I follow it?

Then tell me what the second look found that the first one missed. If it found nothing, look a third time before you say so.

## 6. Traps

The author made each of these mistakes and fixed it. Scan every reflection for them.

- **No distance, time, location, travel or quantities on the nonphysical side.** That's why velocity reflects as "Vibration over Expansion," not a speed, and why the gravity well reflects as a Vortex, an organization in a flow, not a place.
- **No resistance on the nonphysical side.** Talk about receptivity and alignment there. Resistance shows up only as its effect: the gap between where the Current is and what you're living, which resistance on the physical side keeps open (what sets it is Expansion Dilation).
- **Physical words in disguise.** Belief, expectation, will, effort, judgment, morality and rank belong to the physical side.
- **Radiating.** Nothing nonphysical pours outward from a point. If a reflection radiates, it's on the wrong side.
- **Boundaries.** An edge, a line, an inside and an outside are places. On the nonphysical side, find what the boundary does instead: a change in receptivity, a narrowing, a turning.
- **Numbers.** Relationships cross the mirror; quantities don't.
- **Letter echoes** (mass and Movement, force and Frequency) are a pleasant sign, never evidence. Check the meanings.
- **Entity properties on things that aren't entities.** Awareness, Attraction and Momentum arise where the two sides meet in an entity. Don't use them to explain light, a rock or anything else that isn't one.
